# Never Gonna Give You Up — Rick Astley

*Throwback Friday*

It is the most famous bait-and-switch on the internet, but it started life as a perfectly sincere piece of late-80s pop craftsmanship. Written and produced by Stock Aitken Waterman and recorded by a 21-year-old tea-maker from the Pete Waterman studio, "Never Gonna Give You Up" was released in July 1987 and went to number one in twenty-five countries. The video — trench coat, quiff, and that unmistakable baritone, plus a drummer in a chin-stroking stand-off with a pianist — has now been watched well over a billion times on YouTube, most of those clicks arriving years after release via a certain well-known prank.

What keeps it alive isn't the meme, though. It's the chorus: a promise so comically over-committed that it loops back around to being charming. Nobody has ever made devotion sound quite so much like a legally binding contract.

## Chorus

Quoted verbatim from the transcript of the official video:

> Never gonna give you up
> Never gonna let you down
> Never gonna run around and desert you
> Never gonna make you cry
> Never gonna say goodbye
> Never gonna tell a lie and hurt you
